# Build the plan

Building the plan is the fourth step of the planning process. You take the short list of routes from [Weigh the options](/process/weigh-the-options), choose one route for each goal, and write the choices down as instructions: how much goes where, how often, who does it, and by when. The result is a written plan short enough to reread.

## Why it matters

Until now the work has produced facts, goals, and options. None of those moves money. A plan does. It turns "save for the house" into an amount, a schedule, a place, and a name next to each task, so next month you know what to do.

Writing it down is the point. A plan held in your head gets renegotiated every time money feels tight, and nothing shows the drift. A written plan sits still, so when life changes you can see it and decide on purpose.

## What actually happens

### Choosing one route per goal

For each goal, look at the routes on the short list and pick one. The question is which one you will still be following in a year. Where two routes are close, take the one that is easier to undo. Then write down, in a sentence, why you chose it.

### Funding in the order you ranked

The ranked list from [Set goals](/process/set-goals) now becomes the [funding order](/glossary#funding-order), the sequence in which goals receive money. The first goal is funded first, the second from what remains, and so on. The ranking decides which goal gives way when the month is tight. If a goal near the bottom gets nothing this year, the plan should say so.

### Writing the instruction for each goal

Each goal gets one short block with six parts: the goal, the route chosen, how much goes toward it and how often, where that money goes, who does what, and by when. "Emergency reserve: a fixed amount from each paycheck, into an account kept apart from spending money, set up by Ana before the end of the month" is a complete instruction. "Build up savings" is not. If a block cannot be filled in, the route was not really chosen.

### Writing down the assumptions

A plan is not a prediction. A prediction says what will happen. A plan says what you will do, given what you expect, and names those expectations so they can be checked. List the [assumptions](/glossary#assumptions), the guesses the plan rests on: income, the growth you expect on savings, what each goal will cost, and when each is due. Give each a rough figure and a date. When one turns out wrong, you will know which part of the plan to revisit. Checking them is the job of [Stay on course](/process/stay-on-course).

### Keeping it short

The whole plan should fit on a page or two, and you should be able to explain it in minutes. A plan you can explain is one you understand, and one you understand is one you will follow. Detail that does not change what you do next month belongs in the notes.

## Questions to ask yourself

- For each goal, can I say in one sentence why I picked this route?
- If my income dropped, which goal gives way first, and does the plan say so?
- What am I assuming about income, costs, and growth?
- Who does each task, and does that person know?
- Could I explain the whole plan to a friend in five minutes?

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing the best route on paper.** The route with the strongest numbers often demands the most of you each month. Pick the one you will keep.
- **Funding every goal a little.** Spreading the money evenly ignores the ranking. The top goal ends up short and the bottom goals never finish.
- **Mistaking the plan for a forecast.** The plan will not come true as written. Its job is to say what you do next, and to be checkable.
- **Pushing forward when two goals collide.** If two goals cannot both be funded on their chosen routes, do not try harder. Go back a step and [weigh the options](/process/weigh-the-options) again for the lower goal: delay it, shrink it, or pick a cheaper route. A plan that does not add up only moves the collision to the month it happens.

## Which knowledge areas apply

- [Growing your money](/domains/growing-your-money): How the growth you assume for a goal depends on its time horizon and the route's risk.
- [Tax efficiency](/domains/tax-efficiency): Why where the money goes changes how much of it reaches the goal.
- [Retirement runway](/domains/retirement-runway): The goal with the longest horizon and the most assumptions, so it needs the most care.
- [Passing on wealth](/domains/passing-on-wealth): The goals that outlast you, and who does what when you are no longer carrying out the plan.

## For advisors

<Callout type="info">
At this step you are recommending, and the client should be able to see why. For each goal, state the route chosen and the alternatives it beat, in the client's terms. Write the plan in the client's order of priorities, and say which goals are unfunded this year and why. Document every assumption with its figure, source, and date, so the next review can test them. Keep the client-facing plan short; the analysis lives in the file.
</Callout>
