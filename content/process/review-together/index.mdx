# Review together

Reviewing together is the fifth step of the planning process. You take the written draft from [Build the plan](/process/build-the-plan) and walk through it, one goal at a time, with everyone whose life it touches, before anyone acts on it. The result is a plan each person has read and agreed to, or a short list of what to change and which step to change it in.

## Why it matters

A plan is a set of instructions, and instructions only work for people who have read them. This step moves it into every head that has to carry it out, and lets other eyes catch what the author cannot: a dropped goal, a task nobody agreed to, a figure copied wrong.

Changing the plan is cheap here: a sentence crossed out on a page or two of text. Once money has moved, accounts are open, and documents are signed, the same change costs fees, paperwork, and time, and sometimes cannot be made at all. Every objection you surface now is one you do not meet in the middle of [Put it in motion](/process/put-it-in-motion).

## What actually happens

### Deciding who is in the room

Everyone the plan affects belongs in the review, which planners call the [stakeholders](/glossary#stakeholders). That means a partner who shares the money, family members whose lives it touches even if they never touch the accounts, anyone the plan assigns a task to, and a planner if you are working with one. Someone the plan asks to do something, but who has never read it, will not do it.

### Checking each instruction against its goal

Read each block of the plan aloud and ask four things. Does the instruction serve the goal it sits under, or has the route drifted from the reason? Can each person say their own part back in their own words? Does it fit what people will actually do, rather than what they say they will do? And did anything from [Take stock](/process/take-stock) get lost on the way: a debt, a policy, or an account the plan never mentions?

The third question is the hardest. A plan built for a version of yourself that does not exist yet is a plan for someone else.

### Sorting the objections

Objections are the point of the review. Each one is of three kinds, and each kind sends you back to a different step.

| Kind of objection | What it sounds like | Where it sends you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| About the goal | "I never wanted that" | [Set goals](/process/set-goals) |
| About the route | "Not that way" | [Weigh the options](/process/weigh-the-options) |
| About the amount | "We cannot manage that every month" | [Build the plan](/process/build-the-plan) |

Name the kind before fixing anything. An objection about the goal that gets patched by changing the amount comes back next year.

### Reviewing alone

If nobody else is affected, you still need a second reading. Set the draft aside for a few days and reread it as if someone else wrote it. Better, walk a trusted friend through it. Their job is to say back what they heard, so you can hear where the plan is unclear.

### Signing off

The step ends with a clear [sign-off](/glossary#sign-off), a recorded moment when each person says this is the plan they will follow. A date and initials at the bottom of the page is enough, as long as nobody later has to wonder which version they agreed to. If an objection sent you back a step, the sign-off waits for the revised draft.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Who does this plan ask something of, and have they all read it?
- Which instruction assumes a habit I do not have yet?
- Which item from taking stock does the plan never mention?
- For each objection, is it about the goal, the route, or the amount?

## Common mistakes

- **Presenting instead of reviewing.** Asking if the plan is fine produces nods. Asking each person to explain their own part produces a review.
- **Treating silence as approval.** A plan nobody objected to is more likely unread than perfect.
- **Fixing every objection with the amount.** The amount is easiest to change, so it takes the blame for objections that are really about the goal or the route.
- **Skipping the sign-off.** Without a date on the page, the next disagreement is about which version was agreed, not about the plan.

## For advisors

<Callout type="info">
At this step you are presenting your recommendations for challenge, and the client should feel free to challenge them. Walk through each goal in the client's order, state the route and what it beat, and ask the client to explain each instruction back to you before moving on. Record every objection in the client's words and classify it as goal, route, or amount before proposing a change. Document who attended, what changed and why, and which version the client accepted, with the date.
</Callout>
