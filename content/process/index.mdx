# The planning process

Good financial planning follows a repeatable sequence rather than a string of one-off decisions. This section walks through that sequence in seven steps, with one page for each.

## Why a sequence

Money decisions made in isolation tend to work against each other. A fixed order makes sure nothing is skipped, and each step produces exactly what the next one needs.

## The seven steps

1. [Take stock](/process/take-stock): Gather the facts about what you own, what you owe, what you earn, and what you spend.
2. [Set goals](/process/set-goals): Decide what the money is for, how much each goal needs, and by when.
3. [Weigh the options](/process/weigh-the-options): Compare the realistic ways to close the gap between where you are and where you want to be.
4. [Build the plan](/process/build-the-plan): Turn the chosen options into specific, written actions with owners and dates.
5. [Review together](/process/review-together): Walk through the draft with everyone it affects, and adjust before anyone commits.
6. [Put it in motion](/process/put-it-in-motion): Carry out the actions, from opening accounts to signing documents.
7. [Stay on course](/process/stay-on-course): Check progress on a schedule and adapt as your life and the rules change.

## A loop, not a line

The last step feeds back into the first. Every review produces a fresh picture of where you stand, and that picture is the starting point for taking stock again.
