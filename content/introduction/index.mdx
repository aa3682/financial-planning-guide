# Introduction

This guide explains how personal financial planning works, from the first conversation to the ongoing reviews that keep a plan alive. It is free, public, and written in plain English.

## What this guide is

This is an open reference on personal financial planning. It describes the sequence a good plan follows, the knowledge that sequence draws on, and the tools that make the work concrete.

## Who it is for

The first audience is the general public: anyone who wants to understand their finances without a background in the subject. The second audience is practicing financial advisors. Where a topic has practitioner depth, it sits in a clearly marked subsection, so both readers follow the same pages.

## How it is organized

- **The planning process.** Seven steps, in order, from taking stock of where you stand to staying on course over time.
- **Knowledge areas.** Eight bodies of knowledge the process draws on, from money basics to working with a planner.
- **Tools.** Calculators, checklists, and worksheets that support specific steps.
- **Glossary.** Plain definitions of the terms used throughout the guide.

## How to use it

If you are new to the subject, read [the planning process](/process) in order. Each step builds on the one before it. If you have a specific question, go straight to the relevant [knowledge area](/domains). Every area page stands on its own.

<Callout type="info">
This guide is educational. It explains how personal financial planning works in general terms and does not consider your circumstances. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product, or to take any specific action. Tax and legal rules change and vary by jurisdiction; check current primary sources or talk to a qualified professional before acting.
</Callout>
