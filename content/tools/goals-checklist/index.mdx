# Goals checklist

This checklist walks you through the goals your money could serve, helps you give each one an amount and a date, and ends with the three that matter most this year. Print it or copy the tables into a document.

## What this measures

For planning purposes, a goal has three parts: a thing you want, an amount it will cost, and a date you want it by. "Buy a home" is a wish. "Save $60,000 for a down payment within five years" is a goal, because a plan can work out what it takes each month and tell you whether that is possible. The date also gives the goal a [time horizon](/glossary#time-horizon), the number of years between now and when the money is needed, which later decides where the money should sit while it waits.

Writing goals down matters more than it sounds. A goal that stays in your head still steers your spending, but it does so quietly, and it cannot be compared with anything. On paper, goals can be ranked, costed, and checked against each other.

This checklist completes a set of three. The [net worth worksheet](/tools/net-worth-worksheet) shows what you have, the [cash flow worksheet](/tools/cash-flow-worksheet) shows what flows through your household each month, and this page shows what you are aiming at. Together they answer the three questions every plan starts with: where you stand, which way you are moving, and where you want to go.

## Before you start

Have the left-over number from the cash flow worksheet in front of you, because it is what the short list will be measured against. Gather any targets that someone else has already set for you, such as a lender's savings or down payment requirement, a school's published cost estimate, or the payoff figure on a card statement.

It is fine to have goals you cannot fund yet. The list comes first, and the funding comes later.

## Goals to consider

The table below is a prompt, not a form. Read down it and note every goal that applies to you, including the ones that feel out of reach. The categories run in rough life order.

| Goal | What it usually covers | Typical horizon |
|---|---|---|
| **Near-term** | | |
| Cash reserve | Money set aside for a lost job, a medical bill, or a car repair. A planned purchase, such as a car or a trip, belongs in this group too | Under two years |
| **Safety** | | |
| Paying down high-cost debt | Card balances and other borrowing with a high interest rate. Closing an insurance gap, such as missing disability or life cover, belongs in this group too | One to three years |
| **Mid-term** | | |
| A home | A down payment and closing costs. Education costs for you or a child, or a cushion for a career change, follow the same pattern | Three to ten years |
| **Long-term** | | |
| Retirement | Income for the years you will not work, built in a workplace plan and other retirement accounts up to the yearly limits (see [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#retirement-runway)). Giving and helping family belong in this group too | Ten years or more |

A [cash reserve](/glossary#cash-reserve) usually comes first, because it is what keeps every other goal intact when something goes wrong. Safety goals come next, since a debt growing faster than you can pay it or an uninsured risk can undo years of saving. Mid-term and long-term goals are where most of the money eventually goes.

## Your goals

Now write your own list. Give each goal a name, an amount, and a date, and mark its priority as high, medium, or low. High means real harm follows if the goal is missed; medium means life is noticeably worse; low means it would be nice. One example row per category is filled in with made-up values; add rows as you need them.

Be honest about priority, because a want dressed up as a need crowds out the actual needs. If you share finances, each person fills in a copy first and you compare them afterward. The goals you both listed go straight onto the shared list; the ones only one of you listed need a conversation before they do.

## Your short list

Pick the three goals that matter most this year and copy them below. For each, divide the amount by the number of months until the date to get the monthly amount needed. In the example, $9,000 is due in nine months, so the goal needs $1,000 a month.

| Goal | Amount and when | Monthly amount needed |
|---|---|---|
| Cash reserve | $9,000 by next June | $1,000 |
| | | $ |
| | | $ |

Add up the third column and set the total beside the left-over number from the cash flow worksheet. If the total is smaller, the short list can be funded from what you already earn, and the difference can go toward the goals that did not make the list. If the total is larger, you have found a [shortfall](/glossary#shortfall). Something has to give, and the choice is between a later date, a smaller amount, a lower place on the list, or more money coming in.

## What the number does and does not tell you

The short list is a prioritization, not a forecast. It says which goals get money first and what that costs each month. It does not say whether you will reach them, because that depends on income and events you cannot yet see.

The monthly amount is deliberately crude. It ignores growth on the money while it waits, [inflation](/glossary#inflation) in the price of the goal, and any taxes on the way in or out. For a goal a year or two away, those effects are small enough to skip. For a goal decades away, such as retirement, straight division overstates what you need to save from your own pocket by a wide margin, and the plan will replace it with a proper estimate.

Goals compete, and a full list rarely fits within one household's left-over money. That is not a flaw in the list; it is the reason the short list exists. Three goals funded on purpose beat ten goals funded a little each and none finished.

## What to do with it

Revisit the list whenever income, family, or housing changes, and at least once a year even when nothing has. A raise, a move, a new child, or a parent needing care can reorder the whole thing.

Carry the short list into [Set goals](/process/set-goals), where the three parts of each goal are refined and the ranking is settled, and then into [Build the plan](/process/build-the-plan), where each goal gets a route and a written instruction for the money.

## For advisors

<Callout type="info">
A client's own goals checklist is intake for the goals conversation, and the goals left off it are as telling as the ones written down. Lay the completed list beside the net worth and cash flow worksheets and look for conflicts: two high-priority goals due the same year, a planned purchase that would empty the reserve, or a retirement figure with no date behind it. Record each goal in the client's words before translating it into figures.
</Callout>
