# Knowledge areas

The planning process draws on eight bodies of knowledge. Each one has its own section here, written for a general reader, with practitioner depth marked where it appears.

## Why eight areas

Every step of the process needs facts and judgment from several fields at once. Taking stock needs money basics; weighing options needs tax and investment knowledge. Grouping the material into eight areas lets you find one topic without reading the whole guide.

## The eight areas

- [Money basics](/domains/money-basics): Budgeting, saving, borrowing, and why a dollar today is worth more than a dollar next year.
- [Financial defense](/domains/financial-defense): Guarding a plan against the events that can wreck it, such as illness, disability, death, or property loss.
- [Growing your money](/domains/growing-your-money): How investments work, what risk really means, and how to match a mix of investments to a goal.
- [Tax efficiency](/domains/tax-efficiency): How income and gains are taxed, and how timing and account choice change what you keep.
- [Retirement runway](/domains/retirement-runway): Saving for the years after paid work, and turning those savings into income that lasts.
- [Passing on wealth](/domains/passing-on-wealth): Deciding what happens to your assets and dependents if you die or can no longer make decisions.
- [Money and behavior](/domains/money-and-behavior): Why people make the money choices they do, and how habits and biases shape results.
- [Working with a planner](/domains/working-with-a-planner): What a professional does, how they are paid, and the duties they owe you.
